# Completing the run argument with an explicit logarithm bound

15 September 2026. This is part of the `defect_rank_descent_20260915` continuation. The original map, word order, integral sections and first-jet differentials are those of `note.md`. This companion proves two additional all-length results. It does not assert an all-source Collatz conclusion.

## 1. Established transcendence input and the exact specialization

We use the rational-number specialization of Matveev's lower bound, as printed in Theorem 2.1 of Languasco, Luca, Moree and Togbe, *Sequences of integers generated by two fixed primes*, Abhandlungen aus dem Mathematischen Seminar der Universitat Hamburg 95 (2025), 123–148, DOI `10.1007/s12188-025-00293-9`. The complete theorem statement and its logarithmic-height convention were read in the publisher's HTML. That paper cites Matveev's original theorem and the Bugeaud–Mignotte–Siksek formulation. This external transcendence theorem is an explicit proof dependency, not a theorem claimed to have been proved by our finite checker.

In that statement, for positive rational numbers gamma_i, nonzero integers b_i, and nonzero `Lambda=product gamma_i^b_i-1`,

\[
\log|\Lambda|>
-1.4\,30^{k+3}k^{4.5}(1+\log B)\prod_i h(\gamma_i),
\quad B\ge\max_i|b_i|.
\tag{L1}
\]

Here `h(r/s)=max(log|r|,log s)` for reduced positive rationals. We use only `gamma_1=2,gamma_2=3`, with exponents `A,-m`, so their heights are log 2 and log 3. The original coefficient expression is

\[
\Lambda=2^A3^{-m}-1>0,
\quad A>m\ge1,
\quad B=A.
\]

Unique prime factorization proves it nonzero. No exponent in this application is zero. The coefficient in (L1) has the rigorous rational upper bound

\[
1.4\,30^5\,2^{4.5}\log2\log3
<\frac{14}{10}30^5\,24\frac7{10}\frac{11}{10}
=628689600<10^9.
\]

We used `sqrt(2)<3/2`, `1/2<log2<7/10`, and `1<log3<11/10`. The latter inequalities follow from the explicit series bounds in Section 2. Put `C_*=10^9`. Thus the only transcendence estimate used below is

\[
\boxed{\log\Lambda>-C_*(1+\log A).}
\tag{L2}
\]

We retain the original A,m in every substitution. This does not identify a coefficient contraction with an actual descent. The additive correction and integer source congruences enter separately through the run argument in `note.md`.

## 2. A fully rational interval excluding an infinite collection of approximations

For an integer x>=2, set `z=(x-1)/(x+1)`. Integration of the geometric series, or its finite identity with remainder, gives

\[
2\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{z^{2j+1}}{2j+1}
<\log x
<2\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{z^{2j+1}}{2j+1}
+\frac{2z^{2N+1}}{(2N+1)(1-z^2)}.
\tag{L3}
\]

All omitted terms are positive, and replacing their denominators by `2N+1` bounds their sum by the last geometric tail. The implementation uses N=128 for x=2 and x=3. Write the resulting rational intervals as `(l_2,u_2)` and `(l_3,u_3)`. Then

\[
\gamma_-:=l_3/u_2<\gamma:=\log3/\log2<\gamma_+:=u_3/l_2.
\]

The following certificate consists entirely of integer and rational comparisons:

\[
\frac{83130157078217}{52449289519716}
<\gamma_-<\gamma_++2^{-128}
<\frac{350861368503572}{221368876767703}.
\tag{L4}
\]

Its two endpoint numerators and denominators satisfy

\[
350861368503572\cdot52449289519716
-83130157078217\cdot221368876767703=1,
\]
\[
52449289519716+221368876767703
=273818166287419>226\cdot10^{12}.
\tag{L5}
\]

The final endpoint pair is obtained by a mediant search whose steps and hash are recorded; the proof needs only (L4)–(L5), not confidence in that search.

### Elementary denominator lemma

For reduced fractions l=p/q<h=r/s with rq-ps=1, every rational x/y strictly between them, with x,y positive integers, satisfies y>=q+s.

**Proof.** The integers `k=xq-py` and `j=ry-sx` are both positive. Direct expansion gives `y=s k+q j`, hence y>=q+s. This holds even when x/y has not been reduced. QED.

Applying this lemma proves

\[
\boxed{
0<A/m-\gamma<2^{-128},\quad 1\le m\le226\cdot10^{12}
\quad\text{has no integral solution }(A,m).
}
\tag{L6}
\]

The map from the original period coordinates to the rational number is `(A,m)->A/m`. Its fibres consist of integer multiples of a reduced numerator/denominator pair. The denominator lemma applies directly to every unreduced member; no original multiplicity is lost.

## 3. The contracting pure-two comparison is always strictly separated

Recall

\[
D_2(a,b)=2^{a+2b}-3^{a+b},\qquad a,b\ge1.
\]

### Theorem E. Complete positive-gap inequality

On the complete integer domain `D_2(a,b)>0`,

\[
\boxed{D_2(a,b)>3^a-2^a.}
\tag{L7}
\]

**Proof.** The elementary run-cone proof in `note.md` establishes (L7) for all `b>=ceil(3a/2)`. Consider an alleged remaining pair with `0<D_2<=3^a-2^a`. It has

\[
a<b<\lceil3a/2\rceil,\quad
m=a+b<5a/2,\quad A=a+2b<4a.
\tag{L8}
\]

The lower inequality b>a follows from `(2/3)^a(4/3)^b <= (8/9)^a<1` when b<=a. The integer endpoint for b gives the strict A<4a inequality in both parity cases.

The original relative gap obeys

\[
0<\Lambda=D_2/3^{a+b}<3^{-b}<3^{-a}.
\]

Together with (L2) and log 3>1, this yields

\[
a<C_*(1+\log(4a)).
\tag{L9}
\]

It forces a<10^12. For an explicit check, the positive finite sum `sum_(j=0)^100 36^j/j!` exceeds `452*10^12`, so `log(4*10^12)<36`. At a=10^12, the left minus right of (L9) is positive; its derivative `1-C_*/a` is positive thereafter. These inequalities are checked by rational arithmetic.

For a>=128, therefore m<226*10^12 and

\[
0<A/m-\gamma
=\frac{\log(1+\Lambda)}{m\log2}
<2\,3^{-b}/m
<2\,3^{-128}<2^{-128}.
\]

This contradicts (L6). For the remaining 1<=a<=127, the finite certificate computes the least b with D_2>0 and verifies (L7) there. Increasing b sends D_2 to `3D_2+2^{a+2b}`, so the inequality persists. These 127 exact rows cover all remaining b, rather than truncating b at a search bound. QED.

### Corollary E1. Strict original descent at the exact comparison threshold

For every actual positive odd source whose word is `1^a,c_1,...,c_b`, with a,b>=1 and every c_i>=2, the original endpoint is strictly below the original source throughout the domain

\[
\boxed{2^{a+2b}>3^{a+b}.}
\tag{L10}
\]

**Proof.** For a pure-two tail, the exact source parameter and displacement from `note.md` are

\[
3^a t-1=4^b z,\quad z\ge1,
\qquad n-y=2\left(\frac{D_2z+2^a}{3^a}-1\right).
\]

Equation (L7) makes the inner integer at least two, so n-y>=2. A tail containing a higher exponent is handled by the proved monotone affine comparison in Lemma C2 of `note.md`, on precisely the same original source. QED.

This is stronger than the elementary 3/2 cone and the certified 2124/1507 cone. Those earlier arguments remain useful as shorter self-contained certificates that do not use (L1). The new condition refers to the pure-two COMPARISON multiplier; it does not assert that every arbitrary word with contracting actual multiplier descends. The actual higher exponents, offsets, source congruences, and intermediate integers remain attached. A zero displacement left possible by the earlier weak pure-two lemma is now excluded on its actual positive domain by (L7), not deleted from its source declaration.

## 4. A global restriction on the rise beginning at an actual cycle minimum

Let an actual nontrivial positive cycle have primitive odd-return length m, original exponent sum A, and least original vertex n. It cannot have exponent >=2 leaving n, because that would give `(3n+1)/4<n`. Its first a>=1 exponents are therefore a maximal run of ones. The exact original source formula gives

\[
n=2^{a+1}t-1,\qquad t>0\text{ odd}.
\tag{L11}
\]

The common source cover in `note.md` gives n>=s_0=1166401. In the complete original source of this run, the least allowed vertex is more precise than the maximum of two inequalities. It is

\[
t_a=\text{least positive odd integer at least }(s_0+1)/2^{a+1},
\qquad s_a=2^{a+1}t_a-1.
\tag{L12}
\]

This is an exact progression intersection, with inverse `t=(n+1)/2^{a+1}`. For example at a=19, (L12) gives 3145727, not the weaker bound 1166401. At a=17 it gives 1310719. The certificate retains every t_a and s_a.

There is further source information in the already certified supported-zero fibre. The complete equation support E_B in `note.md` has its actual finite integral contraction to 1. A nontrivial cycle cannot contain a vertex of E_B. Define `tilde t_a` to be the least odd t>=t_a for which `2^(a+1)*t-1` is not in E_B, and put

\[
\widetilde s_a=2^{a+1}\widetilde t_a-1.
\tag{L12a}
\]

This exists because E_B is finite. For a<=127 the delivered `retained_zero_minima.json` lists every skipped original parameter and its finite integral first-jet witness. There are only nine skipped sources, with 639 original returns in their witness expansions. Every one is independently checked to belong to the previously constructed E_B with its recorded height. Thus this reuses the retained source behind a supported zero; it is not a new sweep of starting integers or an assertion that a zero value determines its preimage without the map.

For example, at a=17 the original value 1310719 is in E_B and has height 105, although it lies above the contiguous interval bound. Its exclusion makes the next allowed parameter 7 and the next minimum bound 1835007. The unweighted equations and the finite first-jet witness behind that exclusion remain in the record. For larger a the inequality `widetilde s_a>=2^(a+1)-1` suffices; no unknown infinite orbit is queried.


### Theorem F. All-length minimum-run exclusion

Every actual nontrivial positive cycle satisfies

\[
\boxed{m>226a,}
\tag{L13}
\]

where a is the maximal initial exponent-one run at its actual minimum. The coefficient 226 is a proved workbench bound, not a claimed optimal constant or published record.

**Proof.** Assume instead m<=226a. Multiplying the original cycle equations gives

\[
0<\delta:=A\log2-m\log3
=\sum_{i=1}^m\log\left(1+\frac1{3x_i}\right)
\le\frac{m}{3\widetilde s_a}.
\tag{L14}
\]

All x_i>=3, so `2^A<= (10/3)^m<4^m`, hence A<2m<=452a. Also Lambda=e^delta-1 is precisely the positive expression in (L2).

First let a>=128. Equation (L11) gives `delta<=226a/[3(2^{a+1}-1)]<1/2`. The last bound is checked at 128 and persists: `a/(2^{a+1}-1)` decreases, as direct cross-multiplication shows. For `0<delta<1/2`, integration of e^x and `e^{1/2}<2` give `e^delta-1<2delta`. Consequently

\[
\Lambda<226a\,2^{-a}.
\]

Combining with (L2) gives

\[
a\log2<C_*(1+\log(452a))+\log(226a).
\tag{L15}
\]

Since log2>1/2, the rational certificate at a_0=10^12,

\[
\log(452a_0)<36,\qquad a_0/2>37C_*+36,
\quad a_0>2(C_*+1),
\]

and the derivative `1/2-(C_*+1)/a` prove a<a_0. Thus m<226*10^12. On the other hand (L14) gives

\[
0<A/m-\gamma\le\frac1{3\widetilde s_a\log2}<2^{-a}\le2^{-128},
\]

contradicting (L6).

It remains to cover 1<=a<=127. For each such a, the certificate constructs a rational bracket `p_a/q_a < gamma_- < gamma_+ < r_a/s'_a` with determinant one, denominator sum greater than 226a, and

\[
\gamma_++\frac1{3\widetilde s_a l_2}<\frac{r_a}{s'_a}.
\tag{L16}
\]

The exact table contains all 127 source parameters, bounds and endpoint pairs. Equation (L14) places A/m strictly within this bracket. The denominator lemma gives m>226a, the desired contradiction. All rows are checked with exact fractions and the same certified logarithm intervals. QED.

Retaining the original odd parameter first improves the weaker interval-only ratio 191 to 213. The nine original source exclusions in (L12a), already supported by E_B, then improve 213 to 226. The checker verifies both the plain-progression baseline and the support-refined table. No additional discovery source was iterated for this improvement. The previously retained zero fibre is used with its actual original source and boundary witness, not as a bare zero symbol.

### Corollary F1. A complete ordering family of cycles is excluded

There is no nontrivial positive cycle whose primitive original exponent word has all its exponent-one letters in a single cyclic run. The other exponents may be arbitrarily large and their number is unbounded.

**Proof.** Such a cycle has, after its actual minimum, a consecutive ones and then b>=1 exponents >=2. The ones strictly increase each vertex, while every later edge strictly decreases its vertex above 1, so the beginning of the run is indeed the actual minimum and m=a+b. The exponent sum satisfies A>=a+2b=2m-a. The same original cycle product gives

\[
(6/5)^m\le2^a.
\]

Since `(6/5)^4>2`, this forces m<4a, contradicting (L13). A word with no ones decreases at every nonbase vertex and cannot be such a cycle; a word with only ones increases at every vertex. Thus no excluded edge case is assigned a positive periodic source. QED.

This result excludes an all-length family of actual period-torsion modules through their original arithmetic realizations. It does not assert that arbitrary abstract circles with this word pattern are absent. Their rational affine primitives, integral forcing obstructions and supported zero values remain available; what has been excluded is a positive integral cycle realizing that pattern.

## 5. Exact relationship with the first-jet object

No new cohomological quotient is introduced. Every path certified by Corollary E1 uses the same original equations and satisfies

\[
d_\epsilon(\epsilon B_p(n))=\epsilon V_n-\epsilon V_{g_p(n)}.

## 6. Execution and remaining source

`logarithm_certificate.py` uses only exact rational arithmetic. It recomputes the positive series bounds, the large Farey bracket and its determinant, the 127 small pure-run rows, all 127 exact minimum-run source brackets, and the nine reused original supported-zero witnesses. `logarithm_verification.json` records the complete output. `verify.py` includes this certificate and rejects changed bracket endpoints and an invalid coefficient claim. No numerical logarithm or unchecked floating-point continued fraction is a proof premise.

The finite rational certificate does not independently verify Matveev's theorem. The theorem is explicitly sourced; the subsequent finite steps and arithmetic instantiation are checkable here. These all-length results remain additional to, not a rebranding of, the finite-source cover and its 1506-exponent-one-count exclusion.

The actual remaining cycle source has at least two cyclic exponent-one runs and obeys the original minimum-run restriction. The aperiodic source still requires control of genuine packet switches and their resonant source fibres. The nonzero-defect rank in `note.md` handles repeated packets, not arbitrary switching. Neither new argument proves all such sources converge.
