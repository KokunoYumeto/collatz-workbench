# Contributing to the Collatz workbench

This workbench follows the PolyClank idea: retain the mathematics, its motivation,
its development, and the precise checks made when another person or model uses it.
Contributions can be proofs, constructions, computations, formalizations, exposition,
counterexamples, corrected source readings, or justified conjectures.

## Find the mathematics

Use the [reading guide](docs/START_HERE.md) to choose a manuscript and the
[work plan](docs/WORKPLAN.md) to see the current literature and recent-source
strands. Those are starting points, not restrictions on what you can contribute.

Start from the relevant manuscript, not a filename or summary. Identify the exact
source revision and theorem, equation or definition. State the original objects,
coordinates, hypotheses, and maps. When changing a construction, give the comparison
map and specify what it preserves or loses. A finite test should record its bounds,
command and output; an analytical assertion needs its argument.

Describe what you used, what you independently checked, what changed, and what your
check did not cover. Retractions and unsuccessful approaches belong in the history
with their reasons, rather than being silently deleted. A different presentation is
not evidence that two constructions are unrelated.

Keep faithful source exposition separate from independent deductions and preserve
authorship. Do not relabel a practical task as a mathematical open problem. Do not
upload credentials, private conversations, or bulk copyrighted literature. Cite
source works and provide permitted retrieval links instead.

Use an issue for a mathematical discussion or a pull request for a proposed change.
An independent workbench can also publish a scoped check or extension in its own
repository and link it here. No central permission is needed to investigate an idea.
Acceptance of a contribution is not a blanket endorsement of a programme's endpoint.

## Share a workbench, not just a conclusion

1. Open [the repository](https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/collatz-workbench) and
   use GitHub's **Fork** button to create your own copy. Point your AI at that
   copy and the relevant manuscript. Record the starting commit.
2. Keep the argument and its evidence together: readable exposition, editable
   source, citations, proof locations, computations and the exact checks run.
   Make a serious effort to explain the result so another mathematician can
   follow it. Preserve author attribution and record the AI contribution.
3. Publish your work in your fork and, if you wish, leave its link in the
   [PolyClank discussion hub](https://www.reddit.com/r/PolyClank/comments/1wslv90/polyclank_auditing_the_collatz_literature_and/).
   Say which statement or construction it concerns and where to read the
   argument. Ideas and source suggestions can also be shared there.
4. When resuming, read new hub comments and linked workbenches. Compare any
   useful contribution with its sources before importing it, retaining its
   authorship and the checks you actually performed.

A pull request is optional. To offer one, commit the change on a branch of your
fork, then use **Contribute → Open pull request** on GitHub and identify the
target repository and branch. Describe the mathematical change and evidence.
Contributors can continue publishing in their own workbenches without waiting
for a central maintainer; starting a workbench promises no continuing
availability or compute.

Share the LaTeX with the PDF, including the bibliography, figures and build
instructions. If only a source author's PDF is available, keep a checked
transcription explicitly attributed and distinct from editorial corrections.
Respect the source's redistribution permissions. Do not upload protected bulk
literature or private transcripts to make a package self-contained.
